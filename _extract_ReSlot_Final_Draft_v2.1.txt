Computer Engineering Department, Rajarshi Shahu College of Engineering (RSCOE), Pune  |  A.Y. 2026-27
1. Executive summary
Timetables in a college are drawn once and then constantly broken: a lab loses power, a classroom is booked for an
event, a faculty member is absent, an extra lab becomes available. Today the fix is made by hand, one phone call at
a time, and nobody checks whether the fix creates a new clash in another year's timetable. ReSlot turns that into a
controlled workflow.
The coordinator uploads the master timetable each time (every upload is kept as a locked snapshot), then reports
what changed for a given date (rooms or labs unavailable, extra rooms or labs available, faculty unavailable). ReSlot
finds every affected session, proposes a minimal-change reschedule that is checked for conflicts across all years
and divisions, gets it approved, publishes the updated timetable for that date, and sends each faculty member and
student group a personalised WhatsApp message explaining what changed, when and where.

Scope: Computer Engineering department only (SY, TY and Final Year; 8 divisions), single department, single
college.

Master-first: the master timetable is the source of truth and is read-only once active (enforced by the database).
Every disruption becomes a date-specific override: effective timetable = master + published overrides. Anything a
disruption does not touch stays exactly as the master says.

Ripple-safe: a change in one division can affect other divisions and years through shared rooms, faculty and
students, so ReSlot solves the whole day for all years in one model and shows the full blast radius before
approval.

Safety rule: nothing is published or sent unless an independent validator confirms the change introduces no new
room, faculty or student-group clash (the master's own clashes stay visible as a baseline) and a human approves.

Foundation already built: a tested PostgreSQL schema, the department's master timetable transcribed into 312
sessions, and a conflict detector (Section 14).

Effective timetable (date) = master timetable (active version, that weekday) + PUBLISHED overrides for that
date. The following Monday automatically reverts to the master unless another override exists.
A disruption records what happened (A-210 unavailable 09:00-13:15). A schedule override records the response
(move this session, change its room, or cancel it), always storing the original room and time. Keeping the two
separate makes the system auditable and recoverable.
Master-first rules (strict adherence)

Read-only master. Once a version is ACTIVE, the database rejects any edit to its sessions. A correction is a
new version that is reviewed and approved.

Stored as printed. The room and faculty initials are kept exactly as written in the PDF (e.g. NW/JVS, SPP, ML).
The canonical mapping (NW = NAW) is a labelled interpretation shown in the review screen, never a silent
correction.

Freeze everything else. Only sessions hit by a disruption may change; every other session stays exactly as in
the master, including its pre-existing clashes.

The master's own clashes are the accepted baseline. ReSlot never edits or hides them; they stay visible as
pre-existing. Only a clash created by an override blocks approval.

Least departure from the master. Preference order: same time, another room; then same day, another period;
then cancel or flag for a human. Faculty and batch composition are never changed automatically.

Unknown stays unknown. Missing room or initials are flagged for a human decision, never guessed. Each
override keeps the original assignment, and the next week returns to the master automatically.
Uploading the master every time

Each upload becomes a locked snapshot identified by the file's hash. An identical file reuses the existing version;
a changed file creates a new one and shows what changed in the master itself.

Design rules

Master is immutable once ACTIVE. Triggers reject any edit or delete (tested); a corrected upload becomes a
new version, and only one version can be ACTIVE (partial unique index). Raw room and faculty text is stored as
printed.

Sessions, not cells. Lectures attach to a division; practicals to a batch; electives to a cohort; joint classes link
one session to several groups.

Student overlap is computed, not assumed. groups_overlap() walks the group tree: different divisions never
overlap; ancestor and descendant always overlap; branches below a common ancestor are disjoint only if they
share the same partition key.

Intervals are [start, end), so a session ending at 10:00 does not clash with one starting at 10:00.

7. Rescheduling engine
The solver proposes; the validator checks; a human approves. The AI never sits in this path.
Hard constraints (must hold)

A room hosts at most one session at a time (temporary rooms only inside their available window; closed rooms
never used).

A faculty member teaches at most one session at a time and is not marked unavailable.

No student group is in two sessions at once, using the group-overlap rule above.

Suitability: lab sessions stay in labs (capacity and equipment rules can be added per room).

Multi-period blocks (2-hour labs) move as a unit; teaching periods only (no recess or lunch); fixed sessions never
move.

Frozen: any session not affected by a disruption keeps exactly its master time, room, faculty and groups.
Soft objectives (minimised, weighted)

Number of changed sessions (heaviest weight).

Prefer a room change at the same time over a time change, so students' schedules stay stable.

If time must change: same day, nearest free period; prefer the same lab type.

Avoid creating gaps for students and spreading changes over many faculty.
Algorithm

0. Freeze: every session is locked to the master unless a disruption hits it.

1. Detect: affected sessions = master sessions on that weekday whose room or faculty is hit by a disruption
window.

2. Build one whole-day model (all divisions and years). Candidates per session: its master slot, the same time
in another suitable room (including temporary extra rooms), or another free period the same day; for a hit session
also cancel and flag for a human.

3. Solve with CP-SAT: one option per session, room, faculty and student-group occupancy across all divisions,
minimise the weighted cost (unaffected sessions are very expensive to move). Return the best few plans. Section
8 explains how ripples are handled.

4. Explain infeasibility: if no plan exists (e.g. every suitable lab is closed), say which constraint blocks it instead
of failing silently.

5. Validate independently: re-check the complete effective timetable for the date with plain SQL
(effective_conflicts), not solver code, before approval is even enabled. Only NEW clashes block; the master's
own clashes show as pre-existing.
Room types and lab blocks

Separate lists in the input: classrooms and labs are given as two lists (Appendix A) and stored in one rooms
table with a type.

Labs run in fixed 2-hour blocks: 09:00-11:00, 11:15-13:15 and 14:00-16:00 (plus one Friday 15:00-17:00
session in the master). All 123 lab sessions in the master are exactly 2 hours, so a lab closure removes whole
blocks.

Classrooms are booked by 1-hour period. A practical that moves to a classroom keeps its 2-hour block.


One model per date. Load every session of that weekday for all 8 divisions (about 55) and solve them together
with CP-SAT; at this size it takes seconds.

Default to the master. Each session starts at its master slot. Changing a session hit by the disruption is cheap,
changing an unaffected session is very expensive, a fixed session cannot change.

Ripple depth emerges. If a free room or period exists, only the hit session moves (zero ripple). Another division's
session is displaced only when nothing else works, and the solver picks the displacement that disturbs the fewest
people.


If a plan moves more sessions than a set limit, or touches more than a set number of divisions, the HOD must
confirm it.

The coordinator can pin a session (never move it) or forbid a room, then re-solve.

Several disruptions for one date are solved together; if entered one after another, each approved plan becomes
the baseline for the next.

If no plan exists, the system explains which constraint blocks it; it never publishes a partial fix.
